## What it is

Shavuot, "the Festival of Weeks," falls on the 6th and 7th of Sivan, seven weeks after Passover. It marks the day we received the Torah at Mount Sinai. The seven weeks in between are counted day by day, called the Omer.

## How it is celebrated

- **The Ten Commandments.** On the first morning, the whole community stands to hear the Ten Commandments read from the Torah, as at Sinai. Children are encouraged to come.
- **Night of learning.** Many people stay up on the first night of Shavuot to study Torah.
- **Dairy foods.** Cheesecake, blintzes, and other dairy dishes are traditional.
- **Greenery.** Homes and synagogues are decorated with flowers and plants, recalling the greenery on Mount Sinai.
- **Ruth.** The Book of Ruth is read, for Ruth's choice to join the Jewish people.
- **Yizkor.** The memorial prayers are recited on the second day.
