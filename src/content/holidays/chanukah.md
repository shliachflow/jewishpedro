## What it is

Chanukah, the Festival of Lights, begins on the 25th of Kislev and lasts eight days. It commemorates the victory of the Maccabees over the Syrian-Greek rulers who tried to suppress Jewish practice, and the rededication of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem. According to the Talmud, only one day's worth of pure oil was found for the Temple menorah, and it burned for eight days.

## How it is celebrated

- **Lighting the menorah.** On the first night we light one candle, and one more on each following night, using the shamash (helper candle) to light the others. The candles are lit after nightfall, except on Friday, when they are lit before Shabbat candles. We recite blessings and place the menorah where it can be seen from the street, such as a window.
- **Al Hanissim.** A prayer of thanks is added to the daily prayers and to Grace After Meals.
- **Foods in oil.** Latkes and sufganiyot (jelly doughnuts) recall the miracle of the oil.
- **Dreidel and gelt.** Children play dreidel and receive chocolate coins (gelt).
- **Public lightings.** Many communities hold a public menorah lighting, so the light is shared.
