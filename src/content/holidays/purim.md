## What it is

Purim, on the 14th of Adar, celebrates the rescue of the Jews of ancient Persia from Haman's plan to destroy them, as told in the Book of Esther. The name comes from the lots ("purim") Haman cast to choose the date. In a leap year, Purim is observed in the second month of Adar.

## The four mitzvot of Purim

- **Hear the Megillah.** The Book of Esther is read twice, in the evening and again in the morning. Children and adults make noise with groggers whenever Haman's name is read.
- **Mishloach manot.** Send gifts of at least two kinds of ready-to-eat food to at least one friend.
- **Matanot l'evyonim.** Give gifts of money to at least two people in need.
- **Festive meal.** Eat, drink, and celebrate on Purim afternoon.

## Other customs

- **Costumes.** They recall the hidden miracle of the story, where G-d's hand is not openly visible.
- **Hamantaschen.** Triangular filled cookies are the classic Purim treat.
- **Fast of Esther.** The day before Purim is a fast day, remembering the fast Esther and the Jews kept before she approached the king.
- **Al Hanissim.** A prayer of thanks is added to the daily prayers and Grace After Meals.
