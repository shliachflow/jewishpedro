## What it is

Tu B'Shvat, the 15th of Shvat, is the "New Year of the Trees." It marks the point in the year when trees begin a new cycle of growth, and it is the date used for calculating the age of fruit trees for tithing in the Land of Israel.

## How it is celebrated

- **Fruits.** It is customary to eat fruit, especially the seven species that the Torah praises in the Land of Israel: wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives, and dates.
- **A new fruit.** Many people eat a fruit they have not tasted yet this season and say the blessing "Shehechiyanu."
- **Planting.** Planting a tree, or supporting tree planting in Israel, is a widespread custom.
- **A festive day.** Fasting and eulogies are not said, and the Tachanun prayer is omitted.
- **Lessons from trees.** Trees are a classic image in Jewish teaching for growth, roots, and giving fruit.
